# 🎓 Thesis chapter: what the genre owes its examiner

Assumes the core, §1–9 of [WRITING-STANDARDS.md](../../WRITING-STANDARDS.md).
This file holds the writing rules that apply to a thesis or
dissertation chapter alone: what the genre is for, how rare its figures
and numbered equations are, and what its reread looks for. The element
rules are in [figures.md](../figures.md), [mathematics.md](../mathematics.md)
and [tables.md](../tables.md), including the carve-out that has a
thesis chapter write its tables, figure floats and numbered equations
as real LaTeX; this file repeats none of them.

## 🎯 What a thesis chapter owes its reader

Its reader is an examiner: a domain expert reading adversarially,
looking for the claim that outruns its evidence. That shapes every rule
below. Unlike a survey, this chapter **does** take a position, but
every step of the argument must be traceable to something cited, and
the honest statement of a limitation is worth more than the paragraph
that hides it.

Name the reader's starting point first
([WRITING-STANDARDS.md](../../WRITING-STANDARDS.md) §1): what an
examiner in this subfield already knows, so background is recapped
where it is genuinely needed and not where it is condescending.

**The argument serves a research question.** The chapter's narrative
arc argues toward or around the question it serves. The genre boundary
that matters most here: a chapter that only summarises papers in
sequence is a survey with a chapter heading. If the argument toward the
research question is not visible in the section structure, the chapter
is not doing its job ([WRITING-STANDARDS.md](../../WRITING-STANDARDS.md)
§5).

**Terminology is pinned once and held.** The dossier's glossary is
where the chapter's terms are settled, so that neither the chapter nor
a later revision drifts off them; the reread below checks for a term
that shifts.

A thesis chapter's unit for multi-source synthesis is the paragraph
([WRITING-STANDARDS.md](../../WRITING-STANDARDS.md) §11): a body
paragraph closes on two or more citekeys wherever the evidence allows.
It matters most in the chapter most exposed to transcription, related
work, because a paragraph that fuses several sources cannot be a copy of
any one of them.

## 📐 Figures are occasional

A figure earns its place beside the framework, architecture or study
design it captures, when prose would otherwise take a paragraph to
describe it. Figures here are occasional, not routine, and absence is
the default. When one is drawn, the whole of [figures.md](../figures.md)
§10 applies to it. Its native form is the TikZ picture, vector art that
sets at the thesis's own font and line width, which is the reason this
genre draws one at all.

## 🔢 Mathematics: derivations, mostly unnumbered

A thesis chapter derives and states results, so it carries more
mathematics than a survey, and
[mathematics.md](../mathematics.md) §12 governs all of it. Most
displayed mathematics still needs no number: §12 numbers an equation
only if it is standalone, the final step of a derivation, or reused
later. The one that earns a number is most often the closing line of a
derivation the chapter cites again. A numbered equation is introduced
before the display and read in the sentence that follows, like a table
or a figure.

## 👓 The reread

Read the chapter once as the examiner before presenting it
([WRITING-STANDARDS.md](../../WRITING-STANDARDS.md) §6, in its
adversarial form). Check specifically for:

- a conclusion stated more strongly than its cited evidence supports;
- a section that summarises rather than argues;
- notation or terminology that shifts mid-chapter;
- any claim carrying no citation that is not genuinely the author's own
  contribution.

Where you find overreach, weaken the claim rather than adding a
citation that does not quite support it.
